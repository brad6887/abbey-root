## Martha My Dear

Martha My Dear is the newest addition to the rescue, joining the collection on
July 19, 2026. She came from Sam’s Club—or, as it sometimes feels, the Sam’s
orchid warehouse—where she was marked down to $10. I had not planned on
bringing home another orchid, but a healthy pink Phalaenopsis in full bloom for
ten dollars made a convincing argument.

Unlike several of my earlier rescues, Martha arrived in pretty good condition.
Her leaves were firm, her visible roots were plump and healthy, and she still
had a full display of pink flowers. She did naturally shed her oldest bottom
leaf soon after arriving, but the leaf dried and released on its own without
any signs of rot or disease. Her remaining leaves and crown have continued to
look healthy.

Martha is still in her original nursery pot and bark. Everything visible looks
reasonably good, but experience has taught me not to trust what I cannot see in
the center of a grocery-store orchid. There is a good chance a compact nursery
plug is hidden inside the root ball, holding more moisture than the surrounding
bark.

For now, I am leaving her alone and enjoying the flowers. Once the remaining
blooms have faded, I will repot her, inspect the roots properly, remove any
nursery plug I find, and replace the old media with fresh orchid bark.

Martha My Dear may not need the same kind of rehabilitation as some of the
other orchids in the collection. This rescue may be less about bringing a
struggling plant back from the edge and more about making sure a healthy one
stays that way.
