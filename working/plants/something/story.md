## Something

Something started out in the plant clearance section of a Publix grocery store
in Naples, Florida. Her flowers were still hanging on, so she looked
respectable from a distance, but the leaves told the real story. They were
wrinkled and dehydrated, giving her the general appearance of an orchid that
had recently crossed a desert—or at least spent too long waiting for somebody
at Publix to remember the watering can.

She perked up after being repotted and given a proper soak. The upper leaves
became noticeably less wrinkled, and she held onto her flowers for another
couple of months before they finally began to fade. Around the same time, a
tiny new leaf appeared, which was a reassuring sign that Something had plans
beyond merely surviving the clearance rack.

Then came the move from Florida to Texas. Something survived the trip in her
container, which was an accomplishment on its own, but she ended up severely
dehydrated again. Apparently one cross-country crisis was not enough for her
personal growth arc.

Fortunately, she recovered a second time. Over the following months she
steadily produced a healthy new leaf and began growing fresh roots with bright
green tips. She has not made the fastest or most dramatic recovery in the
collection, but she keeps quietly improving—and after surviving Publix
clearance, repotting, dehydration, interstate travel, and then more
dehydration, Something has earned the right to take things at her own pace.
