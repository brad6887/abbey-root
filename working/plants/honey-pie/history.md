# Honey Pie

## 2026-06-14 — Brought Home and Named

### Photos

- Honey_Pie - 1.jpeg

### What Happened

Brought home a large blooming Phalaenopsis from Kroger and named it **Honey
Pie**.

### Observations

- The plant carried a full display of yellow flowers with raspberry veining
  and pink centers.
- Leaves were broad, thick, and dark green.
- Numerous thick aerial roots were visible.
- The plant appeared healthy at acquisition.

### Notes

This was less of a rescue and more of, "I didn't think Kroger deserved to ruin
this one."

The warm yellow and pink coloring inspired the name.

### Lessons

Not every grocery-store orchid has to be in visible decline before it is worth
bringing home.

## 2026-07-26 — Oldest Leaf Begins to Yellow

### Photos

- Honey_Pie - 13.JPG
- Honey_Pie - 14.JPG

### What Happened

The lowest leaf turned yellow while the orchid continued carrying much of its
original bloom display.

### Observations

- Yellowing was limited to the oldest, bottom-most leaf.
- The yellowing was even across that leaf.
- Upper leaves remained thick, firm, and green.
- The crown remained healthy.
- Aerial roots remained plump and silvery.
- Many flowers remained attractive, though the oldest blooms continued to
  fade.

### Notes

The pattern was consistent with normal leaf senescence. The leaf was left
attached so the plant could reclaim its resources naturally.

### Lessons

One evenly yellowing bottom leaf is not the same as a plant-wide decline.
Healthy upper leaves, crown, and roots provide the necessary context.
