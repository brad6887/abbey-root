## Mother Nature's Son

Mother Nature's Son was supposed to be the next project.

He arrived as a young Better-Gro Cattleya in a mesh sleeve, standing tall
enough to be optimistic and planted loosely enough to fall over whenever he
felt like it. Compared with the Phalaenopsis orchids, he wanted more light,
more drying time, and considerably less fussing. The first assignment was
simple: keep him upright and learn to leave him alone.

That became difficult in March, when the roots started looking worse even as a
new green shoot appeared at the base. By April, the old root system had
deteriorated enough to force the issue. I removed the dead roots, repotted him
in fresh bark, charcoal, and perlite, and waited to find out whether the new
growth had enough stored energy to continue.

It did, but not dramatically.

The new lead grew a little at a time. The leaves stayed firm. After the move
from Florida to Texas, Mother Nature's Son came indoors under grow lights
because the new patio was regularly over 100 degrees. He accepted that change
too and kept building the same lead.

On July 12, it was clearly becoming the next pseudobulb. A week later it was
taller. On July 26, a bright white root with a green tip appeared from its
base.

That root was the whole story. The new growth was no longer surviving on what
the older pseudobulbs had saved for it. It was building a root system of its
own.

Mother Nature's Son never staged a dramatic comeback. He spent six months
quietly becoming a plant that no longer needed one.

### Photos

![Mother Nature's Son — 2026-04-12 — Root Cleanup and Repot](/images/plants/mother-natures-son/photo-17.jpeg)

![Mother Nature's Son — 2026-04-12 — Root Cleanup and Repot](/images/plants/mother-natures-son/photo-19.jpeg)
![Mother Nature's Son — 2026-04-12 — Root Cleanup and Repot](/images/plants/mother-natures-son/photo-20.jpeg)
![Mother Nature's Son — 2026-04-12 — Root Cleanup and Repot](/images/plants/mother-natures-son/photo-21.jpeg)
![Mother Nature's Son — 2026-04-12 — Root Cleanup and Repot](/images/plants/mother-natures-son/photo-22.jpeg)

### What Happened

Removed the plant from its old setup, inspected the root system, removed dead
brown roots, and repotted it into fresh open medium.

### Observations

- The old root system was compromised.
- Brown, dead roots required cleanup.
- The new pot contained coarse bark, charcoal, and perlite.
- The active basal growth remained intact through the repot.

### Lessons

The plant needed a clean, airy setup, but the living growths and viable roots
had to be preserved rather than stripped indiscriminately.
