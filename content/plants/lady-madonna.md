## Lady Madonna

Lady Madonna arrived before the record did. I believe she was a $5 Lowe's
clearance orchid bought late in 2025, but I did not start tracking her until
January. By then, the useful part of the story was already clear: she had been
close to dead when I brought her home and was still trying to decide whether
recovery was worth the trouble.

The first photographs were not especially glamorous. She had drooping older
leaves, a lot of stress written across the plant, and roots that needed close
attention. The crown was intact, though, and there was new growth in the
center. That was enough reason to keep going.

Then she produced a spike.

It appeared in mid-January, developed slowly through February and March, and
opened into deep purple flowers in early April. An orchid that had nearly died
had managed a full bloom cycle. For a while, that looked like the ending.

Lady Madonna had other ideas. A move in June left her packed for roughly two
weeks and visibly set her back. The flowers were gone, the older leaves hung
low, and the plant had to settle into another environment. This time the
recovery happened mostly underground. By July, the clear pot showed a dense
root system with several bright green growing tips. The leaves still carried
the cosmetic evidence of the move, but the roots were doing exactly what they
needed to do.

On July 26, a small folded green point appeared in the center of the crown. It
may be the beginning of a new leaf. That identification is still tentative,
but it is the right kind of uncertainty: Lady Madonna is no longer being
watched to see whether she survives. She is being watched to see what she
grows next.
