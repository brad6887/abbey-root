## Phal McCartney

I don’t remember exactly when Phal McCartney joined the group. It was sometime
around the middle of 2025, and I’m fairly sure I rescued him from a Publix.
Apparently my record-keeping system at the time was mostly, “I’ll definitely
remember this later.”

By the time I started properly tracking him in January 2026, Phal was already
doing pretty well. His leaves were firm, his crown looked healthy, and he had
an impressive collection of aerial roots wandering in every direction. The
old, compacted moss in his pot was the main concern, but Phal himself seemed
unconcerned. He had clearly decided that roots were decorative and should be
displayed outside the pot whenever possible.

Then he put on a show.

Beginning in February, Phal produced a huge cluster of purple-and-white
patterned flowers and kept it going well into April. This was not a modest
little recovery bloom. It was a full comeback tour, complete with more flowers
than seemed reasonable for an orchid his size.

Then, in the middle of that run, life became considerably less
orchid-friendly.

When I made my hasty exit from Florida, there wasn’t time for a graceful
transition. I cut off the flowers, packed the orchids into plastic tubs, loaded
the tubs into a U-Box, and shipped the whole group to Texas. They stayed packed
away for about a month while I found somewhere to live and got settled enough
to unpack them.

Some handled the trip well. Some did not make it.

Phal McCartney handled it remarkably well.

The difference between the photos from April 12 and June 21 is dramatic. In
April, he was covered in flowers. By June, the flowers were gone and the plant
looked a little droopy after a month in a dark plastic tub crossing the
country. It wasn’t his most glamorous era, but he was alive, intact, and
already starting a new leaf. Considering what he had just been through, that
felt like a major victory.

Once he was unpacked and back in regular care, Phal quietly went back to work.
Through the summer he concentrated on that new leaf, which steadily broadened,
flattened, and matured. His roots remained healthy, the older leaves stayed
firm, and the aging potting mix continued to look worse than the plant living
in it.

Phal McCartney may have started as a vaguely documented grocery-store rescue,
but he has become one of my most dependable orchids. He bloomed spectacularly,
survived being packed into a box and shipped halfway across the country, and
resumed growing as though the whole thing had merely been an inconvenient tour
delay.

He still sends roots over the side of the pot whenever he feels like it, of
course. Some artistic decisions are apparently non-negotiable.
