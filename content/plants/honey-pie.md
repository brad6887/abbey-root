## Honey Pie

Honey Pie was less of a rescue and more of a case of, "I didn't think Kroger
deserved to ruin this one."

There was nothing especially tragic about her when I brought her home. She was
a large, healthy Phalaenopsis with thick leaves, roots going everywhere, and a
ridiculous number of yellow and raspberry flowers. Kroger had managed to get
her that far. I decided that was enough Kroger involvement.

The name came first. The warm yellow flowers looked like honey until you got
close enough to see all the raspberry veining and the bright pink centers.
Honey Pie fit, and she joined the rest of the Beatles garden on June 14.

Most of her first six weeks were remarkably uneventful, which is a compliment.
The leaves stayed firm. The roots stayed thick and continued growing. The
flowers aged slowly instead of collapsing all at once. She was still carrying
most of the original display in late July, long after I expected a
grocery-store orchid to start giving up.

The first real change came on July 26, when the oldest bottom leaf turned
yellow. Because it was one leaf, evenly yellowing from the bottom while
everything above it remained healthy, it looked like normal aging rather than
a problem. Even then, Honey Pie was still in bloom and otherwise looked almost
exactly as good as the day she came home.

Honey Pie did not need a dramatic recovery. She needed somebody to notice a
good orchid before neglect turned it into one. So far, getting her out of
Kroger may have been the entire rescue.

## 2026-07-26 — Oldest Leaf Begins to Yellow

### Photos

![Honey Pie — 2026-07-26 — Oldest Leaf Begins to Yellow](/images/plants/honey-pie/photo-11.jpg)
![Honey Pie — 2026-07-26 — Oldest Leaf Begins to Yellow](/images/plants/honey-pie/photo-12.jpg)

### What Happened

The lowest leaf turned yellow while the orchid continued carrying much of its
original bloom display.

### Observations

- Yellowing was limited to the oldest, bottom-most leaf.
- The yellowing was even across that leaf.
- Upper leaves remained thick, firm, and green.
- The crown remained healthy.
- Aerial roots remained plump and silvery.
- Many flowers remained attractive, though the oldest blooms continued to
  fade.

### Notes

The pattern was consistent with normal leaf senescence. The leaf was left
attached so the plant could reclaim its resources naturally.

### Lessons

One evenly yellowing bottom leaf is not the same as a plant-wide decline.
Healthy upper leaves, crown, and roots provide the necessary context.
